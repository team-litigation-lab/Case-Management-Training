Dear Mr. Thone:

We acknowledge Keystone Mutual Insurance's tender of its $10,000 bodily injury limit on behalf of Robert Smith at the March 18, 2027 mediation in Doe v. Smith, to which Harbor Point consented in writing, and your client's demand for the $20,000 limit of the UM/UIM coverage of the above policy, under which Mr. Doe qualifies as an occupant of the insured vehicle.

Harbor Point does not dispute coverage. We do not agree, however, as to the amount of damages Mr. Doe is legally entitled to recover. In particular, Mr. Doe sought no medical treatment of any kind for fourteen (14) days after the collision (February 6 to February 20, 2026), and his course of treatment was later interrupted for nineteen (19) days in April 2026. In our evaluation these gaps bear directly on the causal relationship and reasonableness of the treatment claimed, and on the value of the claim.

Accordingly, pursuant to the arbitration provision of the policy's Uninsured Motorists Coverage, Harbor Point hereby makes written demand for arbitration of the amount of damages. Harbor Point proposes that the matter be heard by a single neutral arbitrator, and proposes Laura Whitcomb, Esq., who has confirmed her availability and has no conflict. Please let us know within fifteen (15) days whether your client agrees to Ms. Whitcomb. Each party will bear its own attorney's fees and expenses; the arbitrator's fee will be shared as the policy provides.

Because Cardinal State Mutual's UM coverage applies in excess of ours, we are copying Cardinal State and invite it to join the arbitration so that the amount of damages may be decided once, in one proceeding.

Nothing in this letter waives any term, condition or defense under the policy.

Sincerely,
