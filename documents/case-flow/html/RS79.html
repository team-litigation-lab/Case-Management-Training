Before February 6 I was fine. I had just started at Northgate as a shift lead and I played pickup basketball twice a week at the rec center. I lived with my mom.

My neck hurt from the day of the crash and by the 18th my right hand was going numb. I called out of work that first week, then went back because I had just started the job. Lifting boxes overhead still sets off the tingling in my thumb and first finger. I haven't played basketball since February.

The crash happened at the corner of our house. I see that stop sign every day. On April 2 my mom was driving me home from therapy and we stopped right there and a truck came up Birch. I felt like it was going to hit us again. My heart was pounding and I couldn't breathe and I got out of the car and walked home. After that I couldn't get in a car to go to therapy. I took the bus to work because it doesn't go by the corner. I used the back door. I wasn't sleeping, maybe four hours, and I kept hearing the crunch from the crash. I stopped answering my phone, even when the law office and the therapy place called. I was embarrassed. I didn't know how to say it.

When I finally called back on April 17 it helped to just say it. Dr. Reyes sent me to a counselor through work and I did six sessions. It helped. I can ride with my mom now and I've been past the corner a few times with her. I still tense up at intersections, and I still don't like going out the front door.

I missed four therapy visits in April. It wasn't that I didn't care about getting better. I couldn't make myself get in the car.

I wrote this statement myself. It is true to the best of my knowledge.
