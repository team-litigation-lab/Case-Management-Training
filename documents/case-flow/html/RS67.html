1. Liability

At about 7:10 a.m., Kevin Alvarez was driving his 2020 Ford Escape eastbound on Maple Hollow Road, with Mr. Doe belted in the front passenger seat. Mr. Alvarez came to a full stop at the all-way stop and proceeded. Mr. Smith, northbound on Birch Avenue in a 2017 Chevrolet Silverado, did not stop. He struck the Escape's front passenger door at about 28 mph and pushed it into Mr. Doe's mother Margaret Doe's 2019 Honda Civic, parked at the curb on Birch Avenue.

  • Riverton PD Officer Thomas Garner (#2217) cited Mr. Smith for failure to stop at a stop sign (report RPD-26-018842, Exhibit A).
  • An independent witness, Ruth Calloway of 2209 Maple Hollow Road, gave a signed statement at the scene: the SUV stopped fully and went; the pickup "didn't even slow down" (Exhibit B).
  • Doorbell-camera video from 2214 Maple Hollow Road shows the Escape stopped at the line and the Silverado entering the intersection without stopping. Four stills, reprinted in color from the native file, and the video file are enclosed with its chain-of-custody log (Exhibit C).

Liability is not reasonably in dispute.

3. The interval before first treatment

Mr. Doe first saw a provider on February 20, 2026, fourteen days after the collision. The record shows that his symptoms began at the scene and never stopped:

  • He reported neck stiffness to the paramedics on the date of loss (Exhibit D).
  • On February 7, 2026 he bought ibuprofen and lidocaine patches at Riverton Drug (receipt, Exhibit E).
  • He called out of work at Northgate Distribution from February 9 through February 13, 2026, citing neck pain (Exhibit H), and texted his mother about his neck pain from February 7 through February 15.
  • He had no way to get to a doctor: his mother's Civic was towed from the scene, the Escape was in the shop, and no rental was authorized until February 18, 2026.
  • Numbness reached his right hand on February 18, 2026. He went to urgent care on his first day off, February 20.

His treating orthopedist, Dr. Alana Reyes, has reviewed the records. Her letter of June 19, 2026 (Exhibit G) states, to a reasonable degree of medical probability, that the delayed presentation is clinically consistent with a disc injury whose radicular symptoms evolve over days to weeks, that the C5-C6 herniation was more likely than not caused by the February 6, 2026 collision, and that the 2021 low-back care is unrelated.
