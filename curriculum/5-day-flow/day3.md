### 3.1.1 Time-Limit Demand Mechanics & the Statutory Bad-Faith Setup

> **Lesson** · Day 3 — Demands & settlement — Track A · Module 1: BI & UM Demand Execution  
> **Topic:** What makes a $10,000 demand one Keystone has to answer  
> **Continuity:** John Doe v. Robert Smith · Atty. Elijah Thone · $10,000 BI / $20,000 HOST UM / $10,000 REL UM · 14-Day Gap  
> **Theory extracted from:** `D2#11` Case Phase: Demand — The Legal & Procedural Foundation · `D2#12` Demand: The Policy Limit Mindset · `D2#13` Treatment Gaps in the Demand Phase · `D2#17` Demand: Best Practices · Newly authored: the elements of a time-limited demand that can support a later failure-to-settle claim (within limits, clear and reasonable terms, sufficient documentation, a stated window with proof of delivery)

#### 1 · Visual Layout Description

Left half: a time-limited demand letter on navy-banded letterhead, five orange numbered badges pinned to its parts: ① the $10,000 amount (within limits), ② the release and affidavit terms (clear), ③ the conditions and the 30 days to meet them (reasonable), ④ the enclosure list (documented), ⑤ the deadline and delivery lines (timed). Right half: a horizontal clock bar running 07/20/2026 SERVED → 07/22/2026 RECEIVED (a navy mono pill with a return-receipt icon) → 08/21/2026 WINDOW CLOSES in orange. Above the bar, a red danger callout: “An ambiguous term is an escape hatch.” Bottom strip, a full-width navy band in white type: CLEAR · REASONABLE · WITHIN LIMITS · DOCUMENTED · TIMED. The argument: each badge is a door Keystone could refuse through, and the demand shuts all five.

#### 2 · Slide Body — Tell Them

*Why:* Robert Smith carries $10,000; John Doe's specials alone are $16,401.40. A time-limited demand for the limits makes Keystone decide on the record. Vague, over limits, thin or unproven: a refusal costs it nothing. Clear, reasonable, within limits, documented and timed: an unreasonable refusal can matter.

1. Confirm the foundation first: SOL docketed (02/06/2028); liability (Smith's stop-sign citation, RPD-26-018842; Ruth Calloway; the doorbell video); coverage (BI $10,000, Keystone's dec page, 03/20/2026).
2. Within limits: $10,000, Keystone's full BI limit. A demand above it is one Keystone can't accept within its policy, so refusing it generally sets nothing up.
3. Clear: every term acceptable as written: $10,000; who is released (Robert Smith and Keystone only) and for what (the BI claim only; the Civic's PD claim stays open); a limited release; Smith's affidavit of no other insurance; how to accept and by when; when payment is due after acceptance (Atty. Thone sets it).
4. Reasonable: ask only what Keystone can deliver in time. Its limit, a limited release and its insured's affidavit qualify; 30 days leaves time to get the affidavit. A condition it can't meet, or a short fuse, is its defense.
5. Documented: the Big Four complete, enough to evaluate the claim inside the window. A carrier can't be faulted for refusing what it was never shown.
6. Timed and proven: the letter names what starts the clock (delivery of the certified mailing). Serve by certified mail, return receipt, AND email; log both; docket from receipt: 07/22/2026 + 30 = 08/21/2026 (Fri).
7. Answer the gap first: name the 14 days (02/06 → 02/20/2026), say why (no car until 02/18, cost worry, hand numbness from 02/18), and prove continuity: the EMS report, the Northgate call-outs (02/09–02/13), the urgent-care onset note, Dr. Reyes's causation letter (06/19/2026).
8. Lead causation with the 03/13/2026 MRI: a C5-C6 herniation with “no significant degenerative change at the adjacent levels.” That line answers “degenerative.”
9. Present, don't compile. Demand at MMI (06/12/2026), future care narrated; the 30-Day Rule dockets MMI + 30 (07/12/2026). A later date (07/20/2026) is Atty. Thone's call, reason recorded.
10. Bad faith is a legal conclusion; the rules vary. Some states set a time-limited demand's required terms by statute; missing one hands Keystone a safe refusal. Atty. Thone confirms whether one governs this file. Exposure above $10,000 is Atty. Thone's analysis.

> ⚠️ **UPL boundary.** You build, audit, serve and prove delivery of the demand Atty. Thone approved and signed. You never set or change its terms, grant an extension, call a refusal bad faith to the adjuster, or tell John Doe what the claim is worth or whether to accept.

**Best practices & pitfalls**

- Know which value-drivers the demand leads with: the MRI herniation, the injection, the residual right-arm paresthesia at MMI, clear liability.
- Docket the deadline from the receipt date on the return receipt, never from the mailing date.
- The window is for acceptance, not payment: Keystone accepted by 08/21/2026 and its check arrived 09/04/2026. When payment is due after acceptance is a separate term Atty. Thone sets.
- A well-explained gap costs less than an unexplained one: adjusters use gaps to argue the injury was minor, healed early, was over-treated or came from something else.
- ⚠️ Pitfall: a demand with no stated deadline, or one that doesn't say what starts the clock.
- ⚠️ Pitfall: leaving the gap out of the timeline and hoping Keystone misses it.
- ⚠️ Pitfall: demanding before MMI or a future-care plan exists.

1. BI exhaustion first. Here Host UM opens once Keystone's $10,000 tender (08/12/2026) is documented; exhaustion clauses and their effect vary by policy and state (confirm the governing rule). Notice of both UM claims went out in 03/2026; it's the demand that waits.
2. Prove Keystone is all there is: its dec page (03/20/2026) and Smith's affidavit of no other insurance (08/10/2026). An underinsured claim needs proof of exhaustion, not of no insurance.
3. Send the 30-Day Letter the day after the tender (08/13/2026): notice of Keystone's $10,000 offer to Harbor Point and Cardinal State, asking each to consent and waive subrogation, or substitute its own check to keep its claim against Smith. 30-day windows here; confirm the governing rule.

5. Limit the release to Robert Smith and Keystone, and to the BI claim. Keystone's 08/14 draft freed “all other persons, firms, corporations and insurers,” both UM carriers included. Flag it to Atty. Thone; the limited release was signed 08/28/2026.
6. Add-on or offset decides the stack. Harbor Point confirmed add-on in writing (03/02/2026); offset would leave $10,000 of room. Cardinal State's 03/09 letter confirms coverage, not form: get in writing that no offset or anti-stacking cap applies. $40,000 needs both.
7. Relative UM is excess. The Escape's policy answers first; Cardinal State (Margaret Doe's policy; John Doe covered as a resident relative, 03/09/2026) answers only above the $30,000 from BI and Host UM, up to $10,000. Priority between UM policies is Atty. Thone's call.
8. Each package proves damages above what is already covered: Atty. Thone's valuation less the $10,000 BI for Harbor Point, less $30,000 (BI plus Host UM) for Cardinal State. You assemble the proof; the number is his.
9. Before BI money moves: the tort SOL (02/06/2028) and each UM claim's own deadlines (policy suit and arbitration clauses, any UM statute) docketed as Atty. Thone confirms them; Peach State's $1,860.00 claim current, flagged for his made-whole and common-fund positions.
10. Open each UM demand as a tracked CMS task once the release is signed (08/28): a new package, not the BI demand re-addressed.

#### 2 · Slide Body — Tell Them

*Why:* $40,000 is not John Doe's until every claim on it is found, classified and resolved in writing. Liens are not created equal: a statutory lien bends to a formula, a health plan's reimbursement right to a doctrine, a provider's balance to a negotiation. Classify one wrong and you overpay it or freeze the disbursement.

1. Statutory liens (Medicare, Medicaid, a statutory hospital lien, workers' comp) arise by law and bend least. Contractual claims (a plan's reimbursement clause, an LOP) rest on an agreement.
2. Inventory every claimant, absent ones included. Two liens are asserted: Peach State and Riverton Spine & Rehab. Record each “none” with its source: Medicare (the Day 1 SSDI/BCRC check), Medicaid (verified separately: no state-agency interest), hospital (EMS transport declined), workers' comp, prior attorney.

4. Funding decides the law. A self-funded ERISA plan answers to federal law and its own terms and is very hard to reduce; a fully insured plan is generally subject to state insurance law. Peach State is fully insured: what follows is Atty. Thone's call.
5. Common fund: a lienholder repaid from a fund the firm's work and advanced costs created shares the cost of recovery. Atty. Thone asked for the fee share, one-third: $1,860.00 – $620.00 = $1,240.00 (fee and costs would be $1,860.00 × $13,820.08 ÷ $40,000.00 = $642.63).
6. That work was real: the carriers used the 14-Day Gap to call the injury unrelated; the MRI and Dr. Reyes's causation letter answered it. Peach State did none of it.
7. Made whole: in many jurisdictions a subrogated insurer takes nothing until the client is fully compensated. John Doe's recovery was capped by coverage; whether he was made whole is Atty. Thone's analysis.
8. Comparative fault: if the recovery was cut for the client's fault, the lien takes the same cut (20% at fault, 20% off). On Track A no layer took a fault reduction.
9. Quantum meruit: a client may fire a lawyer at any time; the lawyer is then generally owed the reasonable value of the work done, not the contingency fee. Get the time records; strip clerical hours. No prior attorney here: verify and record it.

### 3.3.3 Pro-Rata Distribution & Medical Bill Reduction

> **Lesson** · Day 3 — Demands & settlement — Track A · Module 3: Combined Lien Negotiations & Practice Lab  
> **Topic:** Audit first, then show the lienholder the math  
> **Continuity:** John Doe v. Robert Smith · Atty. Elijah Thone · $10,000 BI / $20,000 HOST UM / $10,000 REL UM · 14-Day Gap  
> **Theory extracted from:** `D3#21` Increasing the Client's Net · `D3#22` Auditing the Billing Before Asking for a Discount · `D2#40` Finalized Settlement: Final Lien Mitigation (The “Reduction”) · `D2#35` The Execution: Lien Payoff Letters (The “Verification” Paperwork) · Newly authored: the pro-rata distribution rule worked on the BI-only illustration; the pro-rata argument restated on true facts for a fund that covers every lien

#### 1 · Visual Layout Description

Top half: the pro-rata illustration as one long horizontal bar, the $6,179.92 fund, split 23.35% navy (PEACH STATE · $1,443.15) and 76.65% orange (RIVERTON SPINE & REHAB · $4,736.77). A bracket beneath it shows $7,965.00 of liens overhanging the bar's right end in hatched red. At the far right a small card in red: JOHN DOE NETS $0.00. Bottom half: a five-step strip of orange numbered badges, ① Audit ② Correct ③ Calculate ④ Ask with authority ⑤ Confirm in writing, ending in a navy ledger chip, NET SHEET UPDATED. The argument: the math is the leverage, and the audit comes before the ask.

#### 2 · Slide Body — Tell Them

*Why:* Half the job is getting money from the carriers; the other half is keeping it from the lienholders. Every dollar a lien gives up lands in John Doe's net. An honest audit and the pro-rata math move a provider further than any plea for a favor.

1. Audit before you ask. Compare the records to the bill line by line: corrections first, reductions second. Never negotiate a percentage off an inflated bill.
2. Riverton Spine & Rehab billed 34 lines × $185.00 = $6,290.00. The records show 33 visits (16 + 17); the 05/04/2026 line is a duplicate. You negotiate from $6,105.00.
3. Match each line's code to that day's treatment note. Upcoding, unbundling and duplicate dates get a demand for correction, not a request for a favor. No line may fall between 04/02 and 04/21/2026, the disruption.
4. Pro rata: when a fund cannot pay every lien in full and no statute or agreement sets an order, liens of equal priority share what is left after fee and costs in proportion to their amounts. Medicare, a statutory hospital lien or some Medicaid recoveries may take priority; confirm the governing rule. Here: two contractual claims.
5. Had only the $10,000 BI been recovered: $10,000.00 – $3,333.33 – $486.75 = $6,179.92 against $7,965.00 of liens. Peach State $1,860.00 ÷ $7,965.00 (23.35%) → $1,443.15; Riverton $6,105.00 ÷ $7,965.00 (76.65%) → $4,736.77. Compute from the fraction, not the rounded percentage. John Doe nets nothing.
6. Argue on true facts only. On Track A the fund covers both liens: never tell a provider it would take only a share, or that the client will refuse the settlement. The true case: a coverage-capped recovery, fee and costs paid from it, a certain payment now.
7. Common fund (procurement costs): in many states a lienholder must reduce by its proportional share of the fee and costs that created the fund: the retainer's fee percentage (33⅓% pre-suit, 40% once suit is filed) plus its pro rata share of costs. It binds some and persuades others; confirm the rule. Atty. Thone argues it.

**Best practices & pitfalls**

- The four sets before archive: the money trail (itemized checks, transaction IDs, zero balance); proof of delivery (John Doe's signed receipt, lien satisfactions); compliance and identity (a current W-9, not the intake copy, for any 1099 reporting; the payer-disclosure confirmation); the closing memo.
- The payer-disclosure confirmation is John Doe's signed statement that he has disclosed every provider, every bill and every payer (MedPay $5,000.00, Peach State $1,860.00, his own $312.40), so every reimbursement claim is known and the releases' indemnity promise can be kept. It does not say nothing else paid: other sources did, and recovering those bills is generally allowed (collateral source; confirm the governing rule).
- Write the closing memo so a stranger understands the file in two minutes: how it started (a passenger, a 14-Day Gap, a $40,000 stack), how it ended, every discrepancy explained, every exception recorded (or “none granted”). Note that the property-damage claim runs separately and is not closed by it.
- A completion certificate belongs only to repair disbursements: the Civic's repair was paid by Keystone PD directly to the shop and never passed through trust.
- Zero balance proved three ways: the bank statement, the trust journal and John Doe's client ledger agree, and only after all five checks have cleared the bank. An uncashed check is followed up and its funds stay in trust until it clears (confirm the governing reconciliation rule).
- Retention: tag each document by type and apply the firm's schedule (on this training file, 7 years from closing); where a stricter rule applies, it controls. Never destroy financial records before the retention date.
- ⚠️ Pitfall: archiving without John Doe's signed acknowledgment of receipt.
- ⚠️ Pitfall: telling John Doe a late bill is “not our problem.”
